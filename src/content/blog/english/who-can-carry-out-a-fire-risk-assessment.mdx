A fire risk assessment should not be treated as a simple checklist. This can only be performed by a professional equipped with the fire risk assessment skills, knowledge and understanding of the building being assessed. If the fire safety person is competent, they may carry out an assessment themselves; however, for more complex properties, employing a professional fire risk assessor can provide greater peace of mind.

## Who Should Conduct a Fire Risk Assessment?

They are responsible for making sure the premises have an appropriate fire risk assessment in place. This may be an employer, building owner, landlord or anyone else who is in control of the premises. But having fire safety responsibility does not mean that someone has the technical expertise to carry out a detailed evaluation.

A fire risk assessment competent person should have the necessary training and experience to determine hazards, identify who is at risk and suggest reasonable safety measures. How much you actually need to know can vary depending on the size, use and complexity of the property.

## When Should You Use a Professional Assessor?

For simple premises, a suitably trained person may be able to carry out an assessment. However, larger or more complicated buildings can require specialist knowledge. This includes residential blocks, HMOs, commercial buildings and high-rise properties.

An example could be the [fire risk assessment for high-rise buildings](/services/fire-risk-assessment/high-rise-buildings), which will require you to understand in detail the escape routes, fire doors, external walls, compartmentation and residents with special needs. Likewise, a [fire risk assessment for flats](/services/fire-risk-assessment/flats) must take into account common areas and how fire could spread between parts of the building.

An [HMO fire risk assessment](/services/fire-risk-assessment/hmo) also requires an understanding of shared accommodation, escape arrangements and the specific risks associated with multiple occupants.
