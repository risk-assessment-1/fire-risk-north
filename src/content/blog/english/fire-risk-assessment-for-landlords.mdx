You could have two landlords with identical houses on the same street, yet one of them might be breaking the law and risking serious trouble, just because they've got one tenant, while the other has got four. That's the harsh reality. It's the difference between something being sensible good practice and a legal duty that'll get you into trouble if you get it wrong. And it is only when an officer or firefighter from either the fire service or a council inspector visits the place that they know what category it belongs to.

## More Fires but Less Fatalities – What Does It Mean?

The number of incidents attended by fire services in England increased by 5.8% to 641,227 incidents in the period from March last year to March of this year. The total fatalities were reduced from 280 deaths to 254 deaths in the past year. These figures were released by the Home Office.

It looks like all those working alarms and clear escape routes are still doing their job, but rented homes are a special case because tenants rarely know the building and they just bring in portable heaters as soon as winter rolls around. That's exactly why you need an assessment to spot potential fire hazards in rental properties before something gets seriously out of hand.

## Which Rental Properties Actually Need an Assessment?

Firstly, there is the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 regulation, which makes the landlord, freeholder or managing agent responsible for fire safety and applies up to the front door of one private dwelling; thus, no further action should be taken by a person renting a single property to one family. In case there is a property containing two or more domestic premises, including flats over a shop, the situation differs, as all the common parts are covered by the order.

The Fire Safety Act 2021 clearly identifies the structural features to be considered, including walls, balconies, and doors leading to the common parts, and starting in October, a new law was introduced, according to which it is required to document all findings. However, some outdated guidance still says that an assessment is needed only in case there are at least five employees in a building. Thus, figuring out whether an assessment is needed and what it should include is the first step you should undertake as a landlord in terms of your fire safety responsibilities for landlords.

| Property Type                    | Do You Need a Fire Safety Order Assessment? | What Does It Cover?                                                                       |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Single-let house, one household  | No                                          | You've still got all your housing duties to worry about                                   |
| HMO                              | Yes                                         | Common areas and shared escape routes are a big deal                                      |
| Purpose-built or converted flats | Yes                                         | Common areas, structure and external walls are all up for grabs, plus flat entrance doors |
| Flats above shops                | Yes                                         | Each part is assessed, and you need to be working with the other duty holders             |

For anyone with a shared building as a rental, a fire risk assessment for landlords is not just a nice-to-have; it's a legal requirement and needs to be taken very seriously.

## Housing Responsibilities Not Dependent on an Assessment

Apart from the Fire Safety Order, another set of housing landlord fire safety regulations applies to every rental property, starting from the simplest single let. The main obligations related to landlord fire safety requirements in England under those regulations include the following:

- One smoke detector on every inhabited floor.
- A carbon monoxide detector in every room with fixed combustion equipment except for gas cookers.
- Checking the alarms on the first day of each new tenancy.
- Annual check of gas safety conducted by a Gas Safe registered engineer.
- Electrical Installation Condition Report at least once every five years.

## Within a Proper Assessment

The Home Office fire risk assessment for landlords checklist contains five stages, and any competent assessment follows this logic:

- Identification of sources of ignition, fuel, and oxygen.
- Identification of people who may get injured, especially those requiring assistance due to mobility, visual and hearing impairments.
- Evaluation of the risk and its removal or reduction.
- Making a record and developing a prioritised action plan.
- Regular review of the assessment.

A detailed residential fire risk assessment implies the application of these stages to the installation of the alarms, escape route planning, compartmentation, fire doors, emergency lighting, signs and housekeeping. It seems irrelevant that bicycles are left under the staircase during lunchtime; however, they may block the only escape route if there is smoke in the corridor. Fire safety in rental properties requires such a judgement that cannot be provided by an internet template.

## How Evacuation Plans Changed Since April 2026?

The Fire Safety (Residential Evacuation Plans) (England) Regulations 2025 entered into force on 6 April 2026 and introduced a new type of residential fire risk evacuation plan known as [Residential Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans](/blog/april-2026-residential-evacuation-plans), or RPEEPs. These regulations concern residential buildings of 18 metres or seven storeys and higher, as well as buildings of more than 11 metres in height where simultaneous evacuation is being used.

Agreed plans contain the description of assistance required by every resident, and the most important information will be stored in the secure information box in the building for firefighters. This type of residential fire risk evacuation plan will work if the evacuation strategy of the building is clear.

## HMOs and Taller Buildings Have More Responsibilities

Co-housing involves higher risk, since a single neglected pan in a communal kitchen may lock out those asleep on floors above it. A building becomes an [HMO](/services/fire-risk-assessment/hmo) upon the presence of three or more persons forming two or more households living in a building using shared facilities, where mandatory licensing typically starts from five residents. Additional licensing schemes are possible by councils, so helpful fire safety guidance for HMOs tends to come from housing officers in the area. Licence terms generally include interlinked detection systems, FD30S self-closers, emergency lights and fire blankets in shared kitchens.

Multi-occupied buildings also have scheduled duties under the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022, and frequencies given below are regularly misquoted online.

| Building                              | To Check What                                                              | How Often                             |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Buildings with two or more residences | Resident fire safety instructions and fire door instructions for residents | Upon arrival, and thereafter annually |
| Over 11 metres                        | Communal fire doors                                                        | Quarterly                             |
| Over 11 metres                        | Entrance fire doors to flats                                               | Annually, on a best-efforts basis     |
| 18 metres or 7+ floors                | Firefighting lifts and firefighting equipment                              | Monthly                               |

Recording such fire safety checks for landlords in a dated log will provide immediate proof of management for the fire officer when asked.

## Who Will Perform Assessment?

The law calls for a "competent person", that is, a person who has sufficient experience and training to make an objective assessment of the risks in a particular building. Owners of a straightforward property can undertake this task themselves; however, HMOs and converted buildings normally call for a professional fire risk assessor. Hiring an expert in no way shifts responsibility from the landlord, which is why checking the following qualifications in advance is critical:

- Third-party certification, e.g., BAFE SP205.
- Professional indemnity insurance.
- Experience with similar buildings.
- Site visit instead of email form.

## When Should the Review Take Place?

There is no set legal period, because the order requires the review to be carried out "regularly", and when the assessment is no longer valid. An annual review is considered best practice, and any one of the following events will provide a sufficient reason to conduct a new assessment:

- Building changes
- Changes in the layout
- Change from single letting to an HMO
- New tenants requiring further assistance
- An actual fire or close call
- The introduction of new legislation

The long-term fire safety compliance for landlords lies in the reviews, not in the report done on the initial visit.

## The Question Most Landlords Will Never Ask

Those who come under the threat of enforcement are not those who have neglected fire safety, but those who assume that the previous year's certificate still applies to this year's building. Changes that tenants make to the apartment, conversions of lofts or just a door which closes incorrectly may quietly make an adequate building a dangerous one. When you get ready for the next occupancy, walk through that path to safety once again and see if all doors, alarms and lights will last through the night. Your answer will speak louder about rental property fire safety than any certificate in your drawer.

## Conclusion

Fire safety is an important responsibility for every landlord, whether they manage a single rental home, an HMO or a block of flats. Understanding which regulations apply, keeping fire safety equipment maintained and reviewing risks when circumstances change can help protect tenants and reduce the risk of enforcement action.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are Fire Risk Assessments Needed by Single-Let Landlords?

No, under the Fire Safety Order, even though smoke alarms, carbon monoxide detectors, and electrical safety duties are applicable in England.

### Are Landlords Required to Conduct the Assessment by Themselves?

Yes, if they are qualified and the structure is not complex; otherwise, more complicated structures usually require a professional.

### Does the Tenant Need to Be Given Fire Safety Information?

The tenant living in a multi-dwelling property must be given proper information concerning the risk factors, safety measures taken, and the responsible person.

### Is There Any Time Limit for Fire Risk Assessments Done for Landlords?

There is no time limit, but it remains valid as long as it is up to date with respect to the building.
