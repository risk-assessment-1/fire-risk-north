Ask most business owners if they're up to scratch with their fire paperwork and you'll get a confident, no worries answer way too often. Although it may not seem like a big deal, a small rule change dropped into place in 2023 really did turn everything on its head for even the smallest operations. Yet, loads of businesses still plod along with assumptions that are years out of date. This fire risk assessment guide for businesses aims to sort out what the law actually wants from you, who's responsible and where well-meaning businesses can blindly stumble without even knowing it's happening. By the time you get to the final section, you may well want to grab your own assessment and re-read every page.

## What Do You Actually Get Asked in a Fire Risk Assessment?

A fire risk assessment is essentially a thorough review of your premises, to the tune of three pretty basic questions: what could set a fire off, who could get hurt, and are you doing enough to stop it? Figures from the fire services in England show just why these questions are so important: they attended 641,227 fires in the year running up to March 2026. That's a 5.8% increase on the previous year, with a total of 254 people sadly losing their lives as a result, according to the GOV.UK fire and rescue incident statistics.

No two buildings are the same, and therefore no two reports ever should be either. A restaurant's assessment all hangs on things like cooking oils, extractor fans and gas supply, while a warehouse assessment is all about stacked stock, and the long, lonely walk to the nearest exit. If done properly, a fire safety risk assessment is one that actually makes sense, not just something that gets left to gather dust in a drawer.

## The Rules: So What Is the Position in the UK?

In England and Wales, according to Article 9 of the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, it is required that "the person or persons who are responsible for making the decision must make a 'suitable and sufficient' assessment and make it on a regular basis". Each nation of Scotland and Northern Ireland has its own fire safety legislation, so companies working across borders should review their procedures for each of them.

The biggest change of late was an alteration that kicked in on 1 October 2023 under Section 156 of the Building Safety Act 2022. Now, whoever's in charge has to write down all the details found in the assessment and the fire safety arrangements, no matter how small the operation. Gone are the old rules that only came into play when you had over five employees. This change has completely flipped the fire risk assessment regulations, leaving sole traders and small offices with a new workload they never thought they'd be responsible for.

## What Has Grenfell Changed for Mixed Buildings?

Changes following Grenfell mainly concern residential properties, although the provisions apply to any businesses working underneath the flats. According to the Fire Safety Act 2021, within multi-occupied residential buildings, the Order applies to the building, including its external walls, and to the doors of flats.

For buildings over 11 metres, the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022 introduce quarterly inspections of communal fire doors and annual inspections of flat entrance doors. An office or a coffee shop situated underneath the flats shares escape routes with other people, hence the need for overlapping fire risk assessment provisions.

## Who Has the Liability?

The employer in charge of the property is usually deemed a responsible person in most businesses. If there is an absence of the employer at the location, then the owner or occupier and the manager assume responsibility.

## The Five-Step Approach to Follow

Guidelines provided by the government indicate that there is a certain sequence of five steps in a fire risk assessment for offices, and an experienced assessor always follows it:

| Step                              | Description of the Step                            | Practical Aspect                                                                      |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identification of Hazards         | Sources of ignition, fuel and oxygen               | Heaters, packaging, flammable liquids, battery charging stations                      |
| Identification of People at Risk  | Employees, visitors, contractors                   | Lone workers, employees working at night, those who need assistance during evacuation |
| Evaluation and Reduction          | Remove, reduce, and protect                        | Fire hazard separation, check of alarms and exits                                     |
| Recording, Planning, and Training | Documented fire risk assessment and emergency plan | Fire marshals, fire drills, actions with property owners and deadlines                |
| Review                            | Keep the assessment up to date                     | After any renovation, incident, or new process                                        |

Risk scores assist in prioritisation, but common sense can never be substituted in such situations. If there is a blocked fire exit, it has to be removed immediately regardless of a neat matrix indicating that the overall risk level is moderate. Charging stations are another issue that has to be considered; whenever the site provides charging services to e-bikes, scooters or tool batteries, the assessment has to evaluate the place and the environment around it. No fire risk assessment for commercial sites guide would consider anything else nowadays.

## What Is Included in a Comprehensive Risk Assessment?

In addition to risks and occupants, a commercial fire risk assessment for an office building will also include checking the following systems for fire safety:

- Suitable fire detection and alarm coverage for the particular type and purpose of the building.
- Unobstructed evacuation routes with acceptable travel distances and keyless points of exit.
- Functional fire doors with good seals and closers and the correct rating.
- Fire extinguishers and emergency lighting equipment, along with their maintenance records.
- Control of hot work, waste storage and arson.

British Standards such as BS 5839-1, BS 5266-1, and BS 5306-3 are recognised benchmarks of such systems.

The fire risk strategy is developed during the design stage for new builds, renovations and changes of use, and reflects the fire safety considerations.

## The Difference Between DIY and Employing a Fire Risk Assessor

Competence is of greater importance in law than a particular certificate; therefore, a trained owner of one simple unit is able to handle everything by themselves.

| Premises               | Is Self-Assessment Realistic? | Is a Specialist Required?                |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Small, simple office   | Yes, if competent enough      | Not necessarily                          |
| Retail shop            | Maybe                         | It depends on its layout and stock       |
| Warehouse or workshop  | Unlikely                      | Quite often                              |
| Hotel or care facility | Uncommon                      | Yes, as people sleep there               |
| Shop below flats       | Uncommon                      | Yes, usually due to common escape routes |

Even a simple workplace fire risk assessment of an office can be problematic when a common stairwell or basement storage room becomes part of the consideration. The useful indications of competence include BAFE SP205 certification, membership of IFE or IFSM registers, and work according to BS 8674:2025. All this indicates that you are dealing with a competent assessor; however, the law requires competence rather than a particular certificate. Your fire and rescue service authority can offer you advice on the issue but not do the assessment for you.

## Conclusion

A fire risk assessment is more than a legal requirement. It helps UK businesses identify potential fire hazards, protect employees and visitors, and maintain a safer workplace. Whether you manage a small office, retail shop or large commercial building, keeping your assessment accurate and up to date is essential.

Regular reviews, clear evacuation procedures and properly maintained fire safety equipment all play an important role in reducing risks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should A Business That Has Two Members Of Staff Have A Written Assessment?

Yes; from 1st October 2023, all responsible persons within the Order will have to record their assessment in writing.

### How Often Should A Review Be Undertaken?

Undertake regular reviews and post any changes in significant matters; an annual review is common sense.

### Can The Fire Service Undertake The Assessment For You?

Your local fire and rescue authority can help with a fire risk assessment guide for businesses, but it cannot undertake the assessment.

### What Is The Difference Between Fire Strategy And Assessment?

Strategy is developed to ensure fire safety in the design phase, whereas the fire risk assessment for businesses involves the assessment of occupied premises.
