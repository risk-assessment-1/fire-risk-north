A fire door may be perfect-looking yet still fail an inspection. Often, the fault does not lie with the wood itself, but rather it's something minor that can easily escape notice. Almost always, there will be something off with the fire door, whether it's a gap that's too wide or the lack of a screw or a defective closer. Being aware of the fire door inspection failure reasons during inspections is key to identifying these issues before they escalate further.

## Don't Judge a Book by Its Cover

The inspectors are not only evaluating the wooden structure and coating of the fire door but also the whole set of the door, including the leaf, the frame, the seals, the hinges, and the closer. They have to work as a team in order to accomplish their one purpose.

It usually all begins with some very small issue. For instance, it might be a missing screw for the hinge that leads to the door sagging, which results in an increased gap and then in the failure of the seal and the lock to operate properly. This is exactly why the flaws become visible during an inspection despite the good appearance of the fire door. The owner might think that everything is fine with the door, while the inspection report will say the opposite.

However, one thing that should be mentioned is that the owners are not ignorant about it, as, according to Article 17 of the Fire Safety Order, it is their responsibility to ensure proper functioning of the fire safety measures.

## What's the Deal with Gaps?

Gaps around the door are often where fire door inspections go wrong. And it's easy to miss the fact that something is wrong because a door that opens and closes smoothly looks fine. The guidance from [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fire-safety-england-regulations-2022-fire-door-guidance/fire-safety-england-regulations-2022-fire-door-guidance), which is the industry standard, says that gaps should be no more than 4mm around the door leaf. And to be fair, it's not like you'd ever notice.

But the gap at the bottom of the door – that's a different story. That needs to be kept as small as possible without the door getting stuck on the floor.

An uneven gap can also suggest that there's something wrong somewhere else. For example, if one corner has a big gap in it and the other has hardly any, that can mean that the door is sagging or the hinges are shot. You often have to rely on other clues to figure it out.

## Don't Overlook Your Seals

Redecorating can easily damage the intumescent strips and the smoke seals without you realising it. Not that you'd ever notice the paint might interfere with the way the seal works, and it can also hide any damage underneath. Government guidance makes it clear that the seal needs to be in good condition and not touched up with paint and that it needs to be in full contact with the door and frame.

But hard-to-spot damage like that can be a real problem. And if you get the wrong replacements, they might look fine, but they've never been tested, and they might not work properly.

## The Closer That Closes in Seconds

In addition to the closer being a necessary component of a fire door inspection since all other fire door elements are dependent upon its proper performance, the government gives recommendations to conduct a simple test. First, you have to open the door fully and release it; then do the same thing when the door opens to 15 degrees.

In both cases, the door should close and lock itself regardless of any friction from the latch or the floor. The door fails the test if it stops within one inch of the door frame. When we talk about smoke and fire protection, the door stays open.

Closers may become non-working or loosen up completely due to the fact that the residents consider the door too heavy to handle. Sometimes, wedges are used by people in order to keep the door open, thus making it useless as a fire protection device.

## Hinges, Holes and Damages No-One Notices

Hinges carry the entire load of the door leaf. Thus, the lack or looseness of any screws can lead to the dropping of the door. Typically, there are three hinges in use; however, depending on the doorset specification, the number and the type of hinges vary. Repair work must be done in accordance with this specification.

Some other fire door defects include splits in the timber, holes from the previous locks and damage to the door edges. All of them weaken the structure that was tested for fire protection.

## When Someone Changes the Door, or the Wall Behind It?

Letterboxes, door replacement from flat to regular ones or adding vision panels could be implemented by residents unaware of the potential impact on a door that has passed the testing.

It is not only the door that requires attention. In some cases, a damaged frame or wall behind the door could play the same role as a damaged door leaf. Additional cables and pipes installed through the wall during the subsequent constructions could cause ventilation openings, despite an ideal condition of the door.

## Missing Labels: A Problem or Just a Question Mark?

A lack of labels, plugs or certificates could make it impossible for assessors to verify the fire rating of a door, but such information cannot be used as a sign of failure.

As per the current governmental guidelines, the absence of strips and certification does not make the existing door unusable. The fire risk assessment defines the required actions.

Disputes regarding fire door compliance defects usually occur due to documentation. People believe that they have to change their doors in case of any missing label. ## Differences in Flats and Shared Houses' Fire Safety Regulations

There are differences in fire safety regulations with regard to blocks of flats and shared houses. According to the [Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2022/547/regulation/10), specifically Regulation 10, fire safety regulations apply to multi-occupied buildings with two or more dwellings sharing a means of escape.

| Duty                                              | Applies To                                                   | Timing                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Give residents fire door information              | Any building with two or more homes and shared escape routes | When residents move in, then within every 12 months                                  |
| Check communal fire doors, including self-closers | The same buildings, where the top storey is above 11m        | At least every 3 months                                                              |
| Check flat entrance doors, including self-closers | The same tall buildings                                      | At least every 12 months, on a best-endeavours basis, with records of refused access |

The repairs needed must be performed by an adequately skilled contractor. The regular inspections continue to be easy and visual.

## Shared Houses: Same Doors, Harder Life

Regulation 10 applies to blocks of flats, and thus councils inspect shared houses via licensing standards and the Fire Safety Order. Consequently, the [fire door safety in HMOs](/services/fire-risk-assessment/hmo) highly depends on the type of property.

As far as council standards are concerned, Guildford and Lambeth require solid and tightly fitting bedroom doors in small two-storey shared houses. Bedsit properties require 30-minute fire doors with closers and seals. Therefore, the landlords have to establish the exact [fire safety requirements for residential buildings](/blog/april-2026-residential-evacuation-plans).

The tenants' activities may also influence the fire safety inasmuch as they may wedge the doors open for ventilation or park bicycles in the corridors. A quick briefing on the risks involved at moving into the accommodation helps avoid such situations.

## A Habit That Outlasts the Next Inspection

Using a [fire door inspection checklist](/services/fire-door-inspection) on the phone makes the procedure easier. Any person who has undergone the relevant training uses it for performing routine checks. First, test the closer when the door is completely open and then at 15 degrees. Then check the latch, gaps, seals and all screws in the hinges.

Afterwards, perform the inspections of the door leaf, frame and glazing according to the fire door inspection checklist. Check if there are any alterations made and wedges keeping the door open. Document the results together with door reference, inspection date and required action. In case the door fails the check twice or is placed in the proximity of building works, the door has to undergo a special inspection.

## The Last Inch Nobody Logs

A failed inspection report does not automatically imply that the fire door has to be replaced. The replacement will depend on the current state of the door, availability, and fire risk assessment. This explains the situation when two doors with the same defect may end up with different decisions.

Some doors may require the screw to be tightened; others will need a new seal, while the rest cannot be repaired anymore. Pay attention to the last inch of the door closing the next time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a Missing Label Mean a Fire Door Has Failed?

Not automatically. A missing label means the door's rating cannot be confirmed from the door itself. Before deciding on replacement, a competent assessor can check the specifications and any supporting documents.

### Can a Fire Door Fail an Inspection If It Closes Properly?

Yes. A fire door may close correctly but still fail an inspection because of damaged seals, excessive gaps, incorrect hinges or defects in the frame. Inspectors assess the complete doorset to ensure it provides the required fire protection.

### What Should Be Included in a Fire Door Inspection Report?

A fire door inspection report should record the door's location, condition, identified defects and recommended corrective actions. It should also include the inspection date and findings relating to hinges, seals, gaps, frames and closing mechanisms.

### Is an Uneven Gap Enough to Fail a Fire Door?

An uneven gap often points to an underlying problem, such as a dropped door leaf, worn hinges or loose screws. The first step is to identify and correct the fire door inspection failure reasons. The gap can then be measured again against the doorset's own specification.

### Can Painted-Over Seals Be Cleaned and Reused?

Cleaning is rarely the safest option because scraping can damage the strip, while paint may hide how well it still works. Replacing the seal with the correct type that is compatible with the door is the safer approach.

### Who Can Carry Out Routine Fire Door Checks?

Trained caretakers, managers and housing staff can carry out basic visual checks because they do not require special tools. However, repairs, replacements and any work that could affect the door's fire rating should be handled by a competent contractor.
