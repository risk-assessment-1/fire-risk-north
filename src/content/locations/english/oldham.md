Oldham sits high on the hill and grew on cotton. The mills remain, the terraces that housed the workers remain, and the town centre has been rebuilt more than once around them.

Instructions come from landlords, agents, employers and owner-managers across the borough. Mill conversions, town centre shops and offices, HMOs, care homes, schools, warehouses and the small industrial units that fill the gaps between the housing.

Each building is read on its own terms. What could burn, where the smoke would travel, who would struggle to get out and what has to change first.

## Mills on the Hill and Terraces Below

The mills were built for machinery. Open floors, timber beams, cast iron columns and stair towers designed to move goods. Converted into apartments, they keep that structure and gain partitions, locked doors and services that were never part of the original design.

The terraces follow the contours, which means steps, changes of level and escape routes that were difficult even when the house held one family. Add a cellar in use, a loft conversion and a house divided between tenants, and the route out becomes the main question.

The town centre and industrial estates add the commercial patterns. Retail units with storage above the shop, workshops with mezzanines and warehouses with racking that has grown taller over the years.

## Fire Risk Assessment Oldham: What We Look At

The assessor begins with the fabric of the building, because in Oldham the construction usually explains the risk. How the floors are supported, how the stairs are enclosed, where the walls stop and what has been run through them.

Then detection and warning, escape routes and exits, doors, lighting, extinguishers, signage and the evacuation plan. Records are reviewed last, and they often show which of the installed systems has been quietly abandoned.

The result is a judgement on whether the precautions are sufficient, with the reasons set out.

## Common Findings in Oldham

Closers set so hard that doors get wedged. A panel in fault with an empty log beside it. Lighting with failed batteries and no test record. A wall breached for a cable. Storage built into the space beneath the only stair.

In the converted mills the recurring issue is the single stair. In the terraces it is the doors, the cellars and the loft conversions. In the commercial units it is usually the mezzanine that changed the escape strategy without anyone revisiting the assessment.

## Why Fire Risk Assessments Matter in Oldham

The Fire Safety Order places the duty on the responsible person, which in most businesses means the employer or whoever controls the premises. Greater Manchester Fire and Rescue Service enforces it across the borough, and Oldham Council applies licensing conditions to HMOs.

The borough's stock gives inspectors plenty to look at, and enforcement follows risk rather than postcode.

A current report is also what insurers want before they quote. A claim is the wrong moment to discover that the last one described a layout that has since changed.

## The Report and What It Records

The document is written for whoever acts on it, whoever prices the work and whoever inspects the building later.

It records the premises and the scope, the hazards, the people at risk, an evaluation of the existing precautions, the significant findings, prioritised actions and review guidance. Photographs are included where they settle a point.

Plain language throughout.
