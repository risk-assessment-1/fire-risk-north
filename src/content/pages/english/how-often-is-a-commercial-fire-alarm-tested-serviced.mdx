A fire alarm ringing at the start of the week can still conceal the existence of a dead battery, dusty detector or lack of connection to the monitoring centre. Weekly fire alarm tests and six-monthly professional servicing help ensure that the system works when needed.

Fire alarms installed in commercial premises should be tested on site weekly. Commercial fire alarm systems should normally be inspected and serviced by a competent person approximately every six months. BS 5839-1:2025 allows service visits between five and seven months apart. The commercial fire alarm testing frequency and fire alarm servicing intervals should follow these recommendations.

## What Happens During The Test Carried Out Each Week?

One of the manual call points is tested by a competent individual using the test key to ensure that the sounders operate and the right location appears on the panel. The [fire alarm testing](/services/fire-alarm-testing) should be logged after resetting the system.

That weekly log matters later, too. It is exactly the evidence an assessor looks for when checking [what happens after a fire risk assessment identifies problems](/what-happens-after-a-fire-risk-assessment), so keep the records alongside your other fire safety documentation.

## What Does a Six-Monthly Fire Alarm Service Include?

The engineer examines the log book, inspects the control panel, standby batteries and the links to the door holders or lift, and ensures that signals are received at the monitoring centre. Throughout the year, all detectors, call points and sounders must be inspected once; hence, systems are normally serviced in parts. These fire alarm maintenance requirements are covered by BS 5839 fire alarm testing.

## Is Commercial Fire Alarm Testing a Legal Requirement?

Fire safety legislation in England and Wales requires fire detection and alarm systems to be properly maintained and kept in working order but does not specify a time frame within which this is to be done. BS 5839-1 is a code of practice and not legislation but serves as the reference for acceptable maintenance.

## When Should a Fire Alarm System Be Reviewed?

Request a review when there have been many false alarms, refurbishment, or a change in use, since the system will not have its design checked by routine maintenance. Those triggers are the same ones that leave a [fire risk assessment out of date](/what-happens-if-your-fire-risk-assessment-is-out-of-date), so it is worth handling both at once.

It also needs one extra check because the analogue phone network shuts down by 31 January 2027, and some alarm communicators require a digital upgrade. [Emergency light testing](/services/emergency-light-testing) should also be considered at the same visit.
