# What Happens If My Business Does Not Have a Fire Risk Assessment?

As a business owner, you have a responsibility to help keep employees, customers and visitors safe. The purpose of a fire risk assessment compliance check is to identify any fire hazards, the people at risk and what measures need to be taken in order for everyone to be able to exit the building safely. It is a statutory requirement that, in most cases, premises (other than dwelling houses) have a fire risk assessment under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 applying to England and Wales.

## Why Is a Fire Risk Assessment Important?

Without an assessment, you will not know whether your fire alarms and escape routes, or the doors, emergency lighting or firefighting equipment required by those premises, are in place and appropriate for the layout of the building. A fire risk assessment is designed to identify the areas where things are not quite what they should be and deal with issues before they become serious safety concerns.

Fire risk assessment requirements can vary depending on the types of buildings you have in use. So for instance, a [fire risk assessment for offices](/services/fire-risk-assessment/offices) may cover aspects such as workplace occupancy, electrical equipment, escape routes and staff procedures, whereas a [fire risk assessment for flats](/services/fire-risk-assessment/flats) may have to take into account communal areas, escape arrangements and the safety of residents. Also, a [fire risk assessment for high-rise buildings](/services/fire-risk-assessment/high-rise-buildings) is slightly different because of the height and evacuation arrangements, considering the number of people that may be affected.

Industrial properties have unique hazards, so an [industrial fire risk assessment](/services/fire-risk-assessment/warehouses) needs to take the following into account: type of equipment used, electrical systems, storage areas for chemicals and combustible materials, plus manufacturing processes.

Your assessment must take into account employees, visitors and contractors, as well as anyone who may need additional assistance when leaving the building.

## What Are the Risks of Not Complying?

If you do not comply with the requirements of fire risk assessment regulations, you may be open to enforcement action or other legal action. Even worse, if there are no good fire safety measures, people may not escape safely from a fire.

Fire safety rules for businesses are intended to ensure appropriate arrangements for the protection of people. That involves identifying risks, mitigating hazards, developing an evacuation plan and training staff properly.

Businesses also need to understand their fire safety legal requirements. Since 1 October 2023, responsible persons covered by the Fire Safety Order in England and Wales must record their fire risk assessment in full, including its findings, regardless of the number of employees.

## Who Is Responsible for Fire Safety?

It is the responsibility of the person with fire safety to ensure that this is done and that the process remains current. Some of the fire risk assessment duties include identifying, reviewing, and taking action on risks where there are safety hazards.

The fire risk assessment should also be reviewed in case of significant changes/renovations to the building or after a fire incident or near miss.
