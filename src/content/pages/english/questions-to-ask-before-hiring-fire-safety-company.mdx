Before hiring a fire safety company, it is important to check its qualifications, experience and approach to assessments. Asking the right questions can help you choose a competent provider. There are six questions that will differentiate an honest assessor from an expensive liability.

Inquire about their accreditation, other properties they have assessed in the past, their methodology, one of their reports, insurance, and a quote prior to signing on the dotted line. It is your legal responsibility; therefore, you need to ensure that they are capable. And if your existing report has gone stale rather than missing entirely, first check our guide to [what to do if your fire risk assessment is out of date](/what-happens-if-your-fire-risk-assessment-is-out-of-date) — you may need a review rather than a fresh commission.

## Do They Have Proof Of Accreditation?

Fire risk assessors must be competent to carry out suitable and sufficient assessments. Before choosing a provider, check their qualifications, relevant experience and any recognised third-party certification. A reputable [fire risk assessment company North West](/services/fire-risk-assessment) can provide third-party certification of BAFE SP205; all you need is to confirm the number of registrations on the BAFE register since sometimes people give false information. Other registers maintained by the IFSM and Institution of Fire Engineers contain individual assessors.

The same logic applies to servicing work: check for BAFE SP101 before you book [how often commercial fire extinguishers need servicing](/how-often-fire-extinguishers-need-servicing), because the certification sits with the engineer as much as the firm.

## Have They Assessed Any Properties Like Yours?

The risk factors at a care home, a warehouse and an office would be different, so you can ask for case studies of premises similar to yours and their findings. You should expect to answer questions related to your staffing levels, your procedures and your stock before any [fire risk assessment in Preston](/fire-risk-assessments-preston) visits take place.

## What Process Will They Use?

PAS 79-1:2020 is the accepted code of practice for premises other than housing, while BS 9792:2025 applies to housing premises such as flats and HMOs. Be careful of any assessments done by a fire risk assessment company remotely or in the same hour, as a proper [fire risk assessment in Blackpool](/fire-risk-assessments-blackpool) would usually require inspecting the premises.

Ask, too, how the firm handles follow-on compliance work. A provider that understands [how often a commercial fire alarm should be tested and serviced](/how-often-is-a-commercial-fire-alarm-tested-serviced) will flag alarm and detection issues in the report rather than leaving you to discover them at the next service visit.

## What Information Should Be Included In The Report And Contract?

Ask the fire risk assessment company to supply you with a sample report suited to your building. Ask them to provide professional indemnity and public liability insurance certificates, quote a fixed price for their visit and report, and give you information about their assessment process.

A good sample report should show prioritised findings with clear timescales — that is what drives [what happens after a fire risk assessment identifies problems](/what-happens-after-a-fire-risk-assessment). If the report is a vague list, the remedial quotes that follow will be just as vague.
